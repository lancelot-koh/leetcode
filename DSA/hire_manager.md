====================Learning New Domains=============================================================

How do you approach unfamiliar technologies?
How do you learn a new system?

How to enter new domain?

- When I enter a new domain, I usually don’t start with the details.
- First, I try to understand the overall architecture and the main workflow. I want to know how the system works end-to-end.
- Then I look for a small entry point and build something myself. That helps me understand the data flow and how the different pieces fit together.
- I also spend time talking to different people. QA and designers often help me understand the expected behavior and user experience. Product managers help me understand the business goals, priorities, and future direction.
- And of course, I learn from engineers and domain experts who know the system much better than I do.
- I’ve found that combining hands-on learning with learning from others helps me ramp up much faster.

Give me an example.

- When I first joined the MDK project, I didn’t try to learn the whole platform because it was just too big.
- I started by understanding the architecture and main workflow — how an application gets created and eventually runs on a device.
- Once I understood that end-to-end flow, everything else started making a lot more sense.
- Then over time I explored different parts of the platform, including runtime behavior, cross-platform support, and some of the architecture behind it.

- What kept me interested wasn’t really the technology itself. It was taking an observation, exploring different possibilities, and seeing where the idea could go.


====================unfamiliar domains=============================================================
How do you approach completely unfamiliar domains?

- I usually don’t try to learn everything upfront.
When I enter a completely unfamiliar domain, I try not to worry too much about what I don't know yet.
- I usually start by understanding the problem we're trying to solve and why it matters.
- Then I try to understand the main workflow and identify the key people who know the domain well.
- From there, I like to build something small, ask a lot of questions, and gradually develop a mental model of how everything fits together.

I've found that once I understand the problem, the workflow, and the key constraints, I can usually ramp up pretty quickly.
